Waste Sorting @ University

Enhancing students' waste sorting experience through research and design.

Combining ethnographic studies, participatory workshops, and digital solutions.

Overview

Waste Sorting @ University is a research-driven project aimed at improving how students interact with recycling systems on campus.
Over 5 months, we investigated students’ behaviors, frustrations, and mental models around waste sorting. By combining ethnographic research, interviews, and participatory workshops, we designed both physical and digital solutions to make recycling more intuitive, accessible, and engaging.


The Problem

Students struggle to sort waste effectively

Students don’t recycle effectively, not because they don’t care, but because the system fails them. Our observations revealed:

  • Confusing bin placement
  • Unclear labels and instructions
  • Low motivation to follow complex sorting rules

As a result, students often relied on guesswork or ignored the system entirely.

The Research

Understanding real behaviors in context

We conducted ethnographic observations in key campus areas such as gardens, vending zones, and study spaces. This allowed us to capture real-time behaviors and friction points.

We then conducted interviews and focus groups that allowed us to uncover that:

  • Bins were hard to find or inconveniently placed
  • Instructions were unclear or inconsistent
  • Students preferred fast, familiar ways to access information
  • Physical and digital touchpoints needed to work together

These insights informed the creation of personas and user scenarios, grounding the design in real needs.

The Strategy

Designing for clarity and engagement

We translated research insights into personas representing different behaviors and motivations. Instead of forcing correct behavior, we focused on reducing cognitive effort and making recycling effortless.
This shift helped us prioritize:

  • Simplicity over completeness
  • Visibility over instruction
  • Guidance over rules

Workshops & Prototyping

We facilitated a participatory workshop involving 12 students, creating a collaborative and hands-on environment. During the session, participants engaged with the personas created by us to better understand different user perspectives and needs. Building on this, they took part in brainstorming activities, generating a wide range of ideas and potential solutions.
To bring these concepts to life, the students then developed low-fidelity prototypes, using accessible materials such as paper, cardboard, Post-it notes, and LEGO bricks.

This process encouraged creativity, experimentation, and active participation throughout the workshop. This hands-on approach helped validate ideas early and ensured solutions were grounded in user expectations.

The Outcome

Practical, user-centered solutions

We designed an integrated system that improves both understanding and usability:

  • Redesigned bins with clearer labeling and more logical placement
  • Digital wireframes integrated into the UniTrento app
  • Step-by-step waste sorting guidance
  • Quick-access information aligned with students’ habits

The result is a system that supports users in the moment, rather than relying on prior knowledge.

Reflection

Key Takeaways

  • Observing real behavior reveals gaps that surveys miss
  • Co-design leads to more practical and accepted solutions
  • Small usability improvements can drive meaningful behavior change
  • Empathy is not a step, it’s the foundation of effective design
