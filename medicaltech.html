Health · Mobile UX

Medicaltech

Redesigning a Telemedicine App for Elderly Patients.

Making health communication simple, intuitive, and trustworthy.

Timeline

5-day sprint

Team

6 people

My Role

UX Research, Wireframing, UI Design

Users

Elderly Users 60+

Overview

Medicaltech is a 5-day design sprint focused on improving the usability of a telemedicine mobile app and introducing video appointment booking. Working in a team of six, we applied the design thinking process to identify key usability issues and redesign the experience around clarity and accessibility. The result is a streamlined platform where patients can track measurements, request prescriptions, book video visits, and communicate with doctors in one simple flow.

The Problem

Telemedicine wasn't working for elderly users

The app had all the necessary features such as measurements, prescriptions, messaging, and video calls, but users over 60 struggled to complete basic tasks. Buttons were hard to find, labels were unclear, and key actions like starting a video call caused confusion.

As a result, many users preferred calling or messaging their doctor directly. The issue wasn't missing functionality, it was poor usability and misalignment with users' needs.

Research

Observing real users

Together with my teammates, I ran usability tests with four elderly participants using their own medical devices. During think-aloud sessions, we observed how they tried to log in, add measurements, and contact their doctor.

We identified key insights that helped us redesign and refine the telemedicine solution:

  • The "+" button did not clearly communicate that it could be used to add a new measurement.
  • The video call button set the wrong expectation, since users thought it would instantly start a call.
  • The login button was sometimes hidden behind the keyboard, blocking access.
  • When tasks became frustrating, participants fell back on calling, WhatsApp, or email instead of the app.

This raised an important question:

How could we make the app feel as natural as sending a message on WhatsApp?

Strategy

Designing for clarity

We focused on simplifying the navigation and aligning the interface with familiar mental models. Instead of floating action buttons or hidden gestures, we prioritized clear labels, explicit actions, and a communication-first flow.

The Outcome

A simpler, more intuitive experience

  • The app now has only two clear sections: Measurements and Communication, because users only needed two core actions.
  • Every action became explicit, reducing hesitation and errors. The floating action button was replaced with labeled buttons and clear call-to-action areas.
  • The video appointment flow became step-by-step: select doctor, choose date/time, confirm, receive clear notifications.
Impact

A measurable difference

Tasks completed faster

Users completed key tasks, such as logging in, adding measurements, contacting their doctor, more quickly and with less hesitation.

Major blockers removed

Hidden buttons, unclear labels, and misleading actions were eliminated. Simplified navigation created a more intuitive communication flow.

Clear communication

Notifications, calls, and primary actions became immediately understandable. Users felt more in control and less reliant on external tools.

What I Learned

This project reminded me how much we can learn by putting ourselves in the users’ shoes. Empathy helped us uncover friction that analytics alone couldn’t show, especially for older users who needed clearer and more explicit interactions. It also reinforced something I value in my design approach: simplicity and clarity often matter more than following trends. And the earlier we test our ideas, the more we can learn, adapt, and improve the final experience.
